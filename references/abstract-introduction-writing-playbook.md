# Abstract and Introduction Writing Playbook

Use this reference when drafting, rewriting, shortening, imitating, or auditing an empirical management-paper Abstract or Introduction. Apply it together with `introduction-theory-writing-rules.md`; when the user supplies a reference paper, also apply `journal-style-analysis.md`.

## 1. Governing Principles

Write an argument, not a compressed literature list.

Use this shared chain:

```text
observable problem
-> unresolved relationship
-> theoretical explanation
-> empirical design
-> verified findings
-> bounded contribution
```

Require every sentence to perform one identifiable function. Remove a sentence when it merely repeats context, praise, or importance already established elsewhere.

Prefer observable facts over inferred firm traits. Do not describe sampled firms as innovative, transforming, uncertain, forward-looking, constrained, or deceptive unless the design directly establishes that trait.

Separate four epistemic levels:

- Confirmed fact: supported by data, a cited source, or a verified manuscript artifact.
- Model-based interpretation: consistent with the estimated pattern and stated as such.
- Reasonable conjecture: useful for theory or future research but not presented as a finding.
- Unverifiable intent: never infer from a disclosure-capability discrepancy alone.

Use association language for observational designs. Reserve `causes`, `leads to`, `drives`, and `results in` for designs that support causal identification.

## 2. Evidence Lock

Before drafting, create a compact fact sheet:

| Element | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Research relationship | What exact X-Y relationship is examined? |
| Sample | Which firms, country, sector, years, and unit of analysis? |
| Construct definition | What does X mean operationally, and what does it not mean? |
| Model form | Linear, nonlinear, mediation, moderation, or another form? |
| Main result | What signs, regions, or contrasts are actually supported? |
| Mechanisms | Which variables capture which theoretical processes, with what direction? |
| Moderator | Which coefficient is significant, and does it change slope, curvature, or both? |
| Turning point | Is movement estimated, calculated, plotted, or only conjectured? |
| Contribution | Which prior understanding changes because of the evidence? |
| Claim boundary | Association, mechanism-consistent evidence, or causal effect? |

Do not draft around an unresolved answer. When evidence is pending, write a placeholder in the fact sheet rather than filling the gap with plausible prose.

## 3. Reference-Paper Style Extraction

When a reference paper is supplied, analyze structure before writing.

### Abstract profile

Record:

- total words and sentences;
- function of each sentence;
- whether the opening states the question, relationship, setting, or puzzle;
- order of main effect, mechanisms, moderation, and contribution;
- typical verbs and claim strength;
- whether measurement details appear;
- whether the final sentence states implication, contribution, or both.

### Introduction profile

Create a paragraph-function map:

```text
P1 phenomenon/problem
P2 construct definition or central tension
P3 outcome importance
P4 literature synthesis and gap
P5 theoretical lens and proposed explanation
P6 design and measurement preview
P7 findings preview
P8 contributions
P9 roadmap, if the target journal uses one
```

Record citation density, gap placement, transition phrases, paragraph length, and the position of data, findings, and contributions.

Imitate rhetorical moves, not wording. Reuse ordinary academic frames only when they fit the focal evidence. Never borrow distinctive metaphors, substantive claims, sample descriptions, or causal language from the reference paper.

## 4. Abstract Architecture

Write the Abstract after the manuscript's empirical and theoretical claims are stable.

Default result-led structure for an empirical management paper:

1. State the focal relationship and sample.
2. State the central pattern and its balanced interpretation.
3. State the mechanisms and their directions.
4. State the moderator or boundary condition in plain language, when central.
5. State the theoretical implication or contribution.

Use five to seven sentences and approximately 100-180 words unless the journal requires another limit. Prefer six clear sentences over fewer overloaded sentences.

### Sentence budget

| Sentence | Job | Include |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question and setting | X-Y relationship; sample/data |
| 2 | Main result | shape/direction; lower and upper ranges when nonlinear |
| 3 | Mechanisms | process variables and directional pattern |
| 4 | Boundary | moderator effect stated understandably |
| 5 | Evidence boundary | observational/causal qualification if needed |
| 6 | Contribution | precise theoretical or practical implication |

Merge sentences 4 and 5 when the word limit is tight. Omit a mechanism or moderator only when it is peripheral to the paper, not merely to shorten the Abstract.

### Measurement detail rule

Do not insert a measurement phrase such as `using a narrative-capability gap measure` merely because the measure exists. Include measurement detail only when at least one condition holds:

- the measure is a primary methodological contribution;
- the construct cannot be understood without its operational distinction;
- the measure prevents a likely conceptual misunderstanding;
- the target journal's abstracts routinely foreground measurement innovation.

When included, use one compact phrase and avoid formula-level detail.

### Abstract citation rule

Do not include citations in the Abstract unless the journal explicitly requires or permits them for an exceptional reason.

## 5. Abstract Sentence Patterns

Treat these as adaptable frames, not fixed prose.

### Opening

```text
We examine the relationship between X and Y using a firm-level panel of [sample].
We explore how X is associated with Y using [setting/data].
Using [sample and period], we test whether and how X relates to Y.
```

Prefer a direct relationship statement. Avoid openings that assign unsupported characteristics to the sample:

```text
Weak: Firms engaged in uncertain but potentially valuable transformation...
Better: When firms communicate their technology-related plans and activities...
```

### Nonlinear main finding

```text
While limited X may be interpreted as [bounded interpretation], an inverted U-shaped association suggests that excessive X becomes counterproductive.
The results reveal an inverted U-shaped association: Y rises over the lower range of X but declines as X becomes larger.
X is positively associated with Y at lower levels and negatively associated with Y at higher levels.
```

Do not reduce an inverted U-shape to `lower X improves Y`. State that the positive association belongs to the lower range and that the direction reverses over the upper range.

### Mechanisms

```text
Mechanism tests show that M1 initially increases and then decreases as X rises, whereas M2 initially decreases and then increases.
The pattern is consistent with an early [resource/process] benefit followed by [credibility/governance] costs.
M1 captures the external-resource pathway, while M2 captures the internal-governance pathway.
```

Name what each mechanism means. Avoid opaque wording such as `strengthens the lower-range response` unless the specific variable, direction, and range are immediately clear.

### Moderation

```text
Greater Z weakens the positive association at lower X levels and causes the negative association to emerge earlier.
Z reduces the slope of the X-Y relationship across the observed range; the evidence does not indicate a change in curvature.
The significant linear interaction and insignificant quadratic interaction indicate a slope change rather than a curvature change.
```

Choose only the sentence supported by the specification. Do not say `Z strengthens the inverted U-shape` when only the linear interaction is significant.

### Evidence boundary

```text
Because the analysis uses observational panel data, we interpret the findings as robust associations rather than definitive causal effects.
The results provide mechanism-consistent evidence, not a definitive causal decomposition.
```

### Contribution

```text
These findings show that the consequences of X depend on its alignment with verifiable capability.
The study extends [literature] by identifying [process/boundary] through which X becomes organizationally consequential.
The results connect [conversation A] with [conversation B] by showing [precise advance].
```

Avoid ending with `This study enriches the literature and has important implications.`

## 6. Introduction Architecture

Use eight or nine functional paragraphs by default. Combine adjacent functions only when the target journal is materially shorter.

### P1. Observable phenomenon and disclosure/problem tension

Open with behavior that the sample and sources can support. Establish why outsiders face difficulty observing, evaluating, or comparing the focal quality.

End with the central tension, not the research contribution.

### P2. Construct definition and boundary

Define the focal construct using observable attributes. State what it does not establish, especially deception, intent, capability totality, or causal direction.

Explain why a limited level and a large level may differ without yet deriving the full hypothesis.

### P3. Outcome importance and connection

Define the outcome and its organizational foundations. Explain how the focal construct may affect those foundations. Do not merely call the outcome important.

### P4. Evidence-backed literature synthesis and gap

Synthesize by research function:

- conceptual definition;
- antecedents;
- external or organizational consequences;
- mechanisms;
- boundary conditions.

Place citations next to each reviewed claim. Then state what these studies do not explain.

Use this logic:

```text
what prior conceptual work explains [citations]
-> what prior empirical work examines [citations]
-> what adjacent theory explains [citations]
-> unresolved relationship/process/boundary
```

Do not write an unsupported gap paragraph. A sentence such as `Overall, existing research leaves this relationship insufficiently explained` is acceptable only after the paragraph has shown, with citations, what prior streams cover and omit.

### P5. Theoretical lens, mechanisms, and boundary condition

Explain why the selected theory fits the problem. Identify the theoretical actors and process. Preview mechanisms in one or two sentences; reserve full derivation for the theory section.

For signaling arguments, identify sender, observable claim, receiver, less observable quality, verification process, and resource or governance response.

### P6. Data and measurement preview

State sample, period, data source, and focal construction. If using a proxy or benchmark, describe it as partial and observable rather than equivalent to the full latent capability.

### P7. Findings preview

Report the main effect first, then mechanisms, then moderation. State ranges and directions. Distinguish formal tests from descriptive interpretations. Use cautious language appropriate to the design.

### P8. Contributions

Use two or three contributions. For each contribution, state:

```text
prior understanding
-> limitation
-> this study's precise advance
-> why the advance matters
```

Do not relabel a result as a contribution. Explain which theoretical conversation changes.

### P9. Roadmap

Include only when expected by the target journal. Keep it to one sentence or a compact paragraph.

## 7. Introduction Sentence Patterns

### Phenomenon and tension

```text
When firms [observable behavior], they face a basic [disclosure/allocation/governance] challenge.
X can [benefit], yet [relevant quality] remains difficult for outsiders to observe and compare.
Firms must therefore balance [benefit] with the need to [credibility/capability requirement].
```

### Construct definition

```text
Prior research describes [observable pattern] as X [citations].
In this study, X refers to [operationally observable definition].
It does not directly identify [intent or latent quality not observed].
As the discrepancy grows, however, [why reconciliation or verification becomes harder].
```

### Outcome bridge

```text
This pattern may have important implications for Y.
Y reflects a firm's capacity to [construct dimensions] [citations].
A limited X may [bounded process], whereas a large X may [competing process].
The association may therefore change as X increases.
```

### Literature synthesis and gap

```text
Existing research has not fully explained this relationship.
Conceptual studies mainly define X and discuss [risks/processes] [citations].
Empirical research has examined its antecedents, including A [citation], and its consequences, such as B [citation], C [citation], and D [citation].
However, these studies do not examine [exact missing relationship or organizational process].
It therefore remains unclear whether [relationship], why [mechanism], and under what conditions [boundary].
```

### Theory and processes

```text
[Theory] provides a useful explanation.
Firms [observable action], while stakeholders use [evidence] to infer [less observable quality] [citations].
Two mechanisms represent these processes.
M1 captures [external process], whereas M2 captures [internal process] [claim-specific citations].
Z may change how quickly or accurately receivers [acquire/interpret/verify] the relevant information.
```

### Design

```text
We test these arguments using [sample] from [period].
We measure [component] using [source] and [benchmark] using [source/method].
A higher value indicates [precise relative meaning], not [overbroad latent meaning].
```

### Findings

```text
The results show [main pattern].
At lower X levels, [direction]; at higher levels, [direction reversal].
The mechanism tests show [M1 pattern], whereas [M2 pattern].
Greater Z [plain-language moderation result].
The significant [interaction] and insignificant [interaction] indicate [slope/curvature distinction].
```

### Contributions

```text
This study makes three contributions.
First, it connects [stream A] with [stream B] by identifying [link].
Second, it extends [literature] from [existing focus] to [new outcome/process].
Third, it provides [measurement/boundary contribution] while recognizing [explicit limitation].
```

Use standard academic frames freely, but vary sentence rhythm and preserve the paper's own constructs and evidence.

## 8. Nonlinear, Mechanism, and Moderation Language

### Nonlinearity

For `Y = b0 + b1X + b2X²`, describe the marginal association as `b1 + 2b2X`. Do not call `b1` the overall positive effect.

Require:

- the sign and significance of the linear and squared terms;
- formal shape evidence when available;
- lower- and upper-range slopes or defensible range language;
- a turning point within effective support before interpreting it substantively;
- no claim that the turning point is an optimal managerial threshold.

### Mechanisms

State the direction of X-M and M-Y evidence, or the nonlinear pattern of M, rather than saying only `mechanisms are confirmed`.

Use `mechanism-consistent evidence`, `pathway evidence`, or `is consistent with` when the design does not identify causal mediation.

### Moderation of a nonlinear relationship

For:

```text
Y = b0 + b1X + b2X² + b3Z + b4XZ + b5X²Z
```

use:

```text
marginal X association = b1 + 2b2X + b4Z + 2b5XZ
conditional curvature = 2(b2 + b5Z)
conditional turning point = -(b1 + b4Z) / [2(b2 + b5Z)]
```

Interpret carefully:

- Significant `XZ`, insignificant `X²Z`: Z changes the slope/intercept of the marginal-effect schedule but provides no evidence of changed curvature.
- A turning point can still move when `XZ` changes the numerator; calculate it before claiming leftward or rightward movement.
- If `b4 < 0`, higher Z lowers the marginal X association at every X when `b5 = 0`; this weakens a positive lower-range slope and makes a negative upper-range slope more negative.
- State `the negative association emerges earlier` only when calculated conditional turning points or plotted marginal effects support it.
- Do not say `strengthens the lower-range response` without naming the variable, direction, and range.

## 9. Citation Placement and Reference Preservation

Use citations as claim-level evidence.

- Keep ordinary groups at 1-3 references.
- Split 4-5 references across the exact antecedents, outcomes, mechanisms, or theories they support.
- Use more than 5 only for a justified review statement and preferably move the mapping to a literature table.
- Do not remove useful sources merely to reduce citation stacking.
- Preserve the de-duplicated bibliography while redistributing citations to more precise claims.
- Use foundational sources for theory and constructs; use recent studies for the current empirical frontier.
- Verify each gap claim against the cited literature. Do not claim that no study examines a topic until the search supports that statement.
- Use `has not fully explained`, `gives less attention to`, or `remains unclear` when the evidence supports incompleteness rather than total absence.

For formal Word delivery, use verified Zotero items and live fields. Audit item fields, bibliography fields, visible citekeys, empty displays, and reference count before and after the edit.

## 10. Style and Compression Rules

Prefer plain academic English and Chinese.

- Use one principal claim per sentence.
- Prefer subject-verb-object order.
- Keep most English sentences around 15-30 words; use longer sentences only when the logical relation remains immediately clear.
- Keep most Introduction paragraphs around 100-180 English words unless the target-journal corpus indicates another norm.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs; reduce stacked nominalizations.
- Define each construct once and use one stable label thereafter.
- Avoid ornamental adjectives such as `rapidly evolving`, `profound`, `unprecedented`, `complex`, or `potentially valuable` unless they carry verified analytical meaning.
- Avoid repeated `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, and `In addition`; use logical transitions tied to the argument.
- Avoid clauses that force the reader to hold multiple mechanisms, qualifiers, and results at once.
- Use an en dash for paired constructs only when the manuscript style requires it; remain consistent.

Compression order:

1. Remove generic importance claims.
2. Remove repeated definitions and theory previews.
3. Replace literature-by-author lists with thematic synthesis.
4. Remove measurement detail that is not essential to the Abstract.
5. Merge adjacent sentences only when they perform the same function.
6. Keep mechanisms, moderation, evidence boundaries, and precise contributions when they are central.

## 11. Bilingual Equivalence

Preserve the same:

- paragraph functions and order;
- construct boundaries;
- literature claims and citation roles;
- sample, period, measures, and model form;
- lower- and upper-range interpretations;
- mechanism directions;
- moderation meaning;
- evidence strength and contribution boundaries.

Translate meaning, not English syntax. Use shorter Chinese clauses when direct translation would create a long, nested sentence. Do not make the English Abstract more cautious or the Chinese Introduction more causal than its counterpart.

## 12. Failure Modes

Reject or revise drafts that contain:

- an opening based on unsupported sample traits;
- a generic gap without cited prior-research evidence;
- a measure detail included only because it sounds technical;
- an inverted U-shape described as a uniformly positive or negative effect;
- `mechanisms` named without their variables and directions;
- ambiguous moderation language such as `strengthens the response`;
- a curvature claim supported only by a linear interaction;
- a turning-point movement claim that was not calculated or plotted;
- causal verbs unsupported by the design;
- a five-source citation cluster that can be mapped to separate claims;
- contributions that merely repeat the results;
- an Abstract that omits a central mechanism or boundary condition but retains generic context;
- an Introduction that fully derives hypotheses and leaves the theory section repetitive.

## 13. Final Audit

### Abstract gate

- Does sentence 1 state the exact relationship and setting?
- Does the main-result sentence describe the full shape or direction?
- Are central mechanisms named with directions?
- Is the moderator stated in ordinary language?
- Are slope, curvature, and turning-point claims distinguished correctly?
- Is measurement detail essential rather than decorative?
- Is the claim strength consistent with the design?
- Does the final sentence state a precise contribution?
- Is the word count within the target limit?
- Are citations absent unless required?

### Introduction gate

- Does the first sentence describe an observable phenomenon?
- Is the focal construct defined without inferring intent?
- Is the outcome connected to its organizational foundations?
- Does the gap paragraph synthesize cited literature by function?
- Does every literature claim have adjacent evidence?
- Is the exact unresolved relationship, mechanism, or boundary stated?
- Does the theoretical lens map actors and processes without duplicating the theory section?
- Are sample, measurement, findings, and limitations accurate?
- Are nonlinear and moderation interpretations mathematically consistent with the model?
- Does each contribution change a named conversation?
- Are ordinary citation groups limited to 1-3 sources?
- Is the section concise, readable, and free of unsupported adjectives?

### Cross-section gate

- Does the Abstract match the Introduction, hypotheses, results, and discussion?
- Does the Introduction promise only analyses that appear later?
- Do citations support the exact claims for which they are used?
- Are reference quantity and quality preserved without citation padding?
- Are Chinese and English versions substantively equivalent?

If any answer is no, revise before Word export or formal delivery.
